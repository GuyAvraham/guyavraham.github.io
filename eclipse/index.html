01 · The problem

You are about 45 km too far north

Your pin puts you at Boltaña / Aínsa in the Sobrarbe, high in the Pyrenees — and the northern limit of totality passes south of you, roughly through Huesca city toward Lleida. From your valley the Moon will cover ~99.5% of the Sun — and that is still a completely different event from 100%. No corona, no stars, no darkness. Totality is binary: inside the band or you missed it.

The fix: drive south, out of the mountains and into the Ebro plains. About 1½–2 hours puts you deep inside the band on the flat Monegros steppe — which also solves your second problem: a Sun only 5° high needs a table-flat western horizon, and a Pyrenean valley can't give you that.

This is a once-in-a-lifetime alignment for Spain — the last total eclipse visible from the peninsula was in 1905, and this one happens at sunset with the Sun hanging just over the western horizon.

02 · Where the eclipse is

The shadow crosses Spain northwest to southeast

The Moon's umbra makes landfall in Galicia at ~20:26 and sweeps to the Balearics by ~20:33 — the whole country is crossed in about seven minutes. Inside the amber band the Sun goes fully dark for up to ~1 min 48 s near the centerline; near the edges, only seconds.

Schematic map (not survey-accurate). Band ≈ 290 km wide. Boltaña sits ~45 km north of the northern limit — the Pyrenees just miss the show, which is exactly why you drive south.

03 · Where you should sit

Pick your seat: dead-flat western horizon, deep in the band

Two constraints decide everything. (1) Be inside the band — further from the northern edge means longer totality. (2) The Sun will be only ~5° high, bearing 285° (WNW) — half a fist at arm's length above the horizon. Any hill, ridge or building to your west hides the entire show, so leave the mountains: you need open steppe or a west-facing ridge with nothing beyond it.

05 · Sun · Moon · Earth

Why this evening is special

At new Moon on 12 August the three bodies align exactly: the Moon slides between Earth and Sun and its cone-shaped shadow — the umbra — just reaches Earth's surface. Spain catches the very end of the shadow's track across the planet (Arctic → Greenland → Iceland → Iberia), which is why totality here happens at sunset with the Sun nearly on the horizon.

Not to scale. Inside the dark umbra cone you see totality; in the wide penumbra (where Boltaña sits) only a partial eclipse. The umbra's footprint on the ground is the ~290 km amber band on the map.

07 · Safety & kit

Protect your eyes — one exception only

Certified eclipse glasses (ISO 12312-2) for every partial phase — that's all of 19:37→20:30 and 20:31→sunset. Sunglasses, phone screens and exposed film do not count. The only time it is safe to look with naked eyes is the ~1 minute of totality itself. When the diamond ring reappears, glasses go back on instantly.

  • Eclipse glasses for everyone in the car — buy them in Aínsa or Barbastro tomorrow; pharmacies and opticians are selling out.
  • Leave Boltaña by ~16:30–17:00. Mountain roads plus eclipse traffic — give yourself margin, arrive by 19:00.
  • Seat check on arrival: face 285° WNW, hold half a fist at arm's length — the Sun must clear everything on the horizon at that height.
  • Water, snacks, layers (temperature drops noticeably near totality), headlamp for the drive back.
  • Phone: shoot the horizon and the people, not the Sun. Your eyes get the corona; cameras without solar filters get damaged.
  • During totality: 10 seconds for photos, the rest for looking. Notice the 360° orange horizon and the planets.

Missed it? Spain gets a second chance: another total eclipse crosses the south on 2 August 2027. But the forecast says you won't need it.
